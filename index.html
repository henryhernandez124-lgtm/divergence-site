What it is, today

A remote desktop that disappears — the picture is native, the protocol is invisible, and the relay between your machines is yours.

01

Boundless native picture

Hardware-decoded H.264 renders edge to edge through a zero-copy Metal compositor — retina-correct, multi-monitor, no window chrome, no seam. The remote machine looks like the one in front of you.

02

Any machine, any protocol

A native Mac-to-Mac path on ScreenCaptureKit, plus RDP and VNC, converge on one substrate. Every source — input, frames, and audio — flows through a single pipeline with the same calm experience on top.

03

A relay you own

The full session crosses any network through a relay you host yourself — a box you control, not a vendor cloud. It forwards encrypted bytes it can't read, and bridges NATs without a VPN. Your machines, your infrastructure, your rules.

What it's for

A remote desktop is the first thing the substrate does. Not the last.

Every signal — a keystroke, a rendered frame, a gesture, a sensor reading — crosses one typed, gated, audited boundary before it reaches the machine on the other side. That boundary doesn't care whether the signal came from a hand, a protocol, or a model. Which is the entire point: build the boundary once, and the things you can carry across it stop being a list and start being a question of what you point it at.

01

Autonomous systems

Observation flowing out, intent flowing in, gated at a single auditable seam. The architecture that carries a desktop is the architecture that carries a vehicle's perception and control — the same boundary, a different signal class.

02

Medicine, at a distance

Remote presence where every action traces back to its origin, and the system can refuse an action when context demands it. Auditable by construction — the seam that authorizes a click is the seam that authorizes a procedure.

03

Neural signal remapping

A typed intent vocabulary built to be extended to new sources — by design, including a brain–computer interface. Restoring movement is a remapping problem, and the substrate is shaped to carry the map.
